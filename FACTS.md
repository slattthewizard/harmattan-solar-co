# Harmattan Solar Co: FACTS (the only numbers and rules this site may publish)

Compiled 14 Oct 2026 from three research packs in `site-factory/sites/harmattan-solar-research/` (prices.md,
regulation.md, technical.md, each fact with URL and access date). Key shop prices were re-checked by curl on 14 Oct
2026 (Mercury Direct 3.5 kVA and 5 kVA packages, Sun King PowerHub, SMK panel list): all matched.
If a figure is not here, do not write it. Write around it, or say an installer will quote it.

## 0. FRAMING RULES THAT BIND EVERY NUMBER

1. A shop price is what ONE named seller lists, never "the price in Nigeria". Always name the seller and the month:
   "Mercury Direct listed a 3.5 kVA lithium package at N3,400,000 in October 2026". Never present a figure as this
   site's quote.
2. Naira prices move with the exchange rate. Mobility.com.ng (June 2026): "A panel priced at N120,000 today can jump to
   N140,000 a month later simply because of currency fluctuations." Every price section says: check a current written
   quote.
3. Never blend sources into one "average cost". Safe summary line: "Published October 2026 package prices from Lagos
   sellers put a 3.5 kVA lithium system with panels at roughly N3.1 to N3.4 million, and a 5 kVA-class system with
   10 kWh of lithium and panels at about N5 to N5.5 million."
4. Package contents differ (battery kWh, panel count, installation included or not). Compare like with like and say
   what each package contains.
5. Write naira as N3,400,000 or N3.4 million (or with the ₦ sign). Watts, kW, kVA, kWh used correctly.

## 6. GRID TARIFFS AND THE STATE SHIFT

- NERC bands by minimum supply: Band A 20 hrs/day, B 16, C 12, D 8, E 4 (NERC Order 2025/064, Ikeja Electric).
- Band A: N209.50/kWh since 1 July 2024 in NERC-regulated areas (NERC orders, latest read May 2026: IE 2026/044, PHED
  2026/048; AEDC 2025/059). It was N225 from April 2024 and N206.80 from May 2024.
- Bands B-E are frozen at December 2022 rates and differ by DisCo (e.g. Band B non-MD N62.48 at Ikeja Electric,
  N63.35 at AEDC). Never quote one national B-E rate.
- Under the Electricity Act 2023 (June 2023) states can regulate their own electricity markets; more than a dozen
  have taken over. Lagos: LASERC took over retail tariffs on 4 June 2025; a LASERC official (Guardian, Sept 2026)
  still quoted Band A at N209.50/kWh and said it is designing "a Lagos-specific tariff methodology". Enugu: the state
  regulator cut Band A to N160/kWh from 1 Aug 2025 (Nairametrics, 21 Jul 2025); no later check. Always say "check your
  own bill / your state regulator".
- Band A feeders that miss their hours are downgraded or compensated (NERC orders; NERC directive on Band A
  compensation for Feb-Mar 2026 shortfalls caused by gas supply and vandalism, reported June 2026).
- City to DisCo (for city pages): Lagos = Ikeja Electric (north/west) and Eko (south), now LASERC-licensed; Abuja =
  AEDC; Port Harcourt = PHED; Ibadan = IBEDC; Benin City = BEDC; Enugu = EEDC area, MainPower in Enugu State.
- Grid collapses: the national grid collapsed repeatedly in 2024 (about a dozen times by press counts) and twice in
  January 2026 (23 and 27 Jan).
- Government statements in July and September 2026 said no grid tariff rise was planned. Never promise future prices.

## 7. SELLING POWER BACK (NET BILLING)

- NERC Net Billing Regulations 2026 (announced 3 June 2026, Vanguard; Daily Trust 9 June 2026): eligibility starts at a
  renewable system of 50 kWp (to 1.5 MWp), with DisCo approval, bidirectional meter and an export tariff "to be
  determined by the Commission". A home system is far below 50 kWp. Say plainly: there is no scheme for a typical home
  to sell power back to the grid. Never say a homeowner can sell power.
- Small systems for your own use sit well under the size at which NERC requires a generation licence or captive
  permit (Electricity Act 2023, s 63 as summarised by commentators). Do not quote section text; never say "no permit
  needed" as legal advice.

## 8. INSTALLER CHECKS AND SAFETY

- NEMSA (Nigerian Electricity Management Services Agency) guidelines as reported in April 2026 (TheNigeriaLawyer,
  20 Apr 2026): "The installation of the rooftop solar PV system must be carried out only by NEMSA-certified electrical
  contractors." Reported items: competency certificate on site, load assessment first, sound roof and mounts, DC and AC
  isolators, breakers/fuses and surge protection, earthing, batteries ventilated and away from living areas and heat,
  lithium batteries with a battery management system, cables in conduit. Reason given: fires from poor workmanship,
  substandard materials and missing protective devices. Attribute as "NEMSA guidelines as reported in April 2026".
- What a buyer can check: NEMSA's site has a Directory of Certified Electrical Contractors (nemsa.gov.ng/directory/) and
  a verification page (nemsa.gov.ng/verification/). Advise: ask the installer for their NEMSA competency certificate and
  look them up.
- Counterfeits: industry experts (NAN via Peoples Gazette, 8 Jul 2026) say batteries, inverters, panels, charge
  controllers, cables and connectors are the most counterfeited, fake batteries and inverters are the biggest fire
  risk; check manufacturer, serial numbers, QR codes and warranty papers, buy from authorised dealers. These are
  industry views, not a regulator warning.
- SON (Standards Organisation of Nigeria) launched standards for solar panels, batteries, inverters and controllers in
  2021 (Blueprint, 18 May 2021). SONCAP is SON's mandatory conformity programme for regulated imports, MANCAP for
  locally made products (son.gov.ng). Do not say which solar goods are on the regulated list; give no standard numbers.
- Consumer law: the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2018 gives a right to return goods that do not
  match the agreed specification for a full refund (s 122, as quoted by Templars, June 2025). Advise: get the exact
  models, warranty terms and VAT treatment written on the quote and keep receipts.

## 9. TAX AND DUTY

- The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 took effect 1 Jan 2026; the standard VAT rate stays 7.5% (Forvis Mazars, Mar 2026). Solar
  equipment is reported to be VAT-exempt or zero-rated under it unless the Finance Minister directs otherwise (sources
  disagree on the label). Safe line: "ask your installer to show the VAT treatment on the quote." No section numbers.
- Panels are reported duty-free and VAT-free at import (NAN, 20 May 2025). Do not state duty on batteries or inverters.
- A ban on solar panel imports was proposed in April 2025 (pv magazine); no ban in force was found by October 2026.

## 12. HARMATTAN AND DUST

- Harmattan is the dry, dusty season carried by dust from the Sahel and Sahara; NiMet bulletins cite dust plumes from
  Niger and Chad, haze reaching the north first and the south later and more lightly. Academic work at Ilorin treats
  November to March as the harmattan season. Give no fixed NiMet dates.
- Soiling studies (worst cases, not typical losses; cite as written):
  - Maiduguri field test (Abdulkarim et al., 2022): "more than 50% loss in power output ... under 14 days of continuous
    deposition"; weekly cleaning advised in intense dust.
  - Calabar dry-season test (Oyom et al., 2023): dust cut the sunlight reaching the panel by 21.1%.
  - West Africa modelling (Isaacs et al., Applied Energy, 2023): soiling losses "can exceed 50%" in the worst-hit
    locations during the harmattan.
  - No measured soiling figure exists for Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Ibadan, Benin City or Enugu. Never quote a
    "typical" harmattan loss for those cities.
- Panel heat: Jinko Tiger Neo datasheet temperature coefficient of Pmax about -0.29%/degC. DERIVED: a cell 30 degC above
  25 degC loses about 8.7% of rated power.
