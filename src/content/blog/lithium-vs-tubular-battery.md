## Tubular Battery Price in Nigeria: Cheaper Per kWh, Shorter Life

Two 220Ah tall tubular batteries wired for 24 V give about 5.28 kWh on the label, for N520,000 at the October 2026 listings above. Four of them wired for 48 V give about 10.56 kWh for N1,040,000. On the shelf, the pair is less than half the price of 5 kWh of lithium, and the four come to roughly half the N1,950,000 to N2,350,000 listed for 10 kWh. That is why tubular is still everywhere.

The catch is life. BusinessDay reported in February 2026 that lead-acid batteries last only three to five years. Lithium makers claim far more cycles, though you should always name the model and call it "manufacturer-stated," never "tested."

- Felicity LPBF48100-A (51.2 V, 5 kWh LiFePO4): its manual states 6,000 cycles at 80% depth of discharge (0.2C, 25C), with a 5-year warranty.
- Luminous LPT 1240H tubular, from an Indian market page: up to 1,500 cycles at 80% depth of discharge.
- Pylontech US5000: datasheet shows more than 8,000 cycles at 25C and a design life of 15+ years, but its warranty document says 5 years of product warranty, with "up to 15 years" as an expected life under the performance warranty.

Dividing 6,000 by 1,500 gives four times the cycles, each maker testing on its own conditions. The cost per unit of energy you actually get out tells a different story from the shelf price. A Felicity 5 kWh at 80% depth of discharge delivers 4 kWh a cycle, so 6,000 cycles is 24,000 kWh. At Nature Solar's N1,200,000, that is N50 per kWh delivered.

Now take a hypothetical 220Ah tubular that matched the Luminous claim. At 80% depth it gives 2.11 kWh a cycle, so 1,500 cycles is about 3,168 kWh, and N260,000 divided by that is roughly N82 per kWh. Lithium comes out cheaper per kWh delivered on paper, but this is a lab-claim illustration with two different batteries. Heat, partial charging and real use will cut both numbers.

One more reality check on the lithium figure. With a 5-year warranty and one cycle a day, you would use about 1,825 cycles inside the warranty, roughly 30% of the 6,000 claimed. The rest is a bet on the battery outliving its paperwork.

## Lithium vs Tubular Battery: The 10-Year Arithmetic

Here is a fairer way to compare: how many times you buy over ten years. This uses today's listed prices (a tubular pair at N520,000, a 5 kWh lithium at N1,150,000 to N1,200,000), and prices will move, so it is an illustration only.

- Tubular pair replaced every 3 years: 4 purchases (years 0, 3, 6, 9), N2,080,000.
- Tubular pair replaced every 4 years: 3 purchases, N1,560,000.
- Tubular pair replaced every 5 years: 2 purchases, N1,040,000.
- 5 kWh lithium lasting the full 10 years: 1 purchase, N1,150,000 to N1,200,000.
- 5 kWh lithium replaced at year 5: 2 purchases, N2,300,000 to N2,400,000.

Now divide N1,200,000 by what a tubular pair costs per year. At N520,000 every 3 years (about N173,000 a year), the lithium price equals about 7 years of tubular spending. At every 5 years (N104,000 a year), it equals about 11.5 years. Same caveat: listed prices, worked arithmetic, not a promise.

So lithium wins on money only if it survives well past its 5-year warranty. Nobody can promise that, and a warranty is the only thing in writing. What lithium also buys is less fuss: no topping up with water, a higher stated depth of discharge (95% in the Felicity manual) and fewer replacement days.

The sums leave out labour for swaps, the water and the lifespan differences from heat. They also assume the battery matches your inverter, which brings us to the examples.

## Checks Before You Pay for a Lithium or Tubular Battery

Fake batteries are a real risk. Industry experts told NAN in July 2026 (via Peoples Gazette, 8 July 2026) that batteries, inverters and controllers are among the most counterfeited solar items, and that fake batteries and inverters are the biggest fire risk. Those are industry views, not a regulator warning. Check the manufacturer, serial numbers, QR codes and warranty papers, and buy from authorised dealers.

Put the following on the written quote, and keep the receipts:

- The exact battery model, voltage and label kWh.
- The depth of discharge the maker states, and the cycles claimed at that depth.
- The warranty length, what it covers, and who honours it. Do not mix a design-life claim with a warranty: Pylontech states 5 years of product warranty and 15 years only as expected life.
- How VAT is treated. Solar equipment is reported to be VAT-exempt or zero-rated under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, but sources disagree on the label, so ask the installer to show it on the quote.

On the installer: NEMSA guidelines as reported in April 2026 say rooftop solar must be installed only by NEMSA-certified electrical contractors. The reported checks include a competency certificate on site, a load assessment first, lithium batteries with a battery management system, and batteries kept ventilated and away from living areas and heat. Ask for the certificate and look the installer up in [NEMSA's directory of certified electrical contractors](https://nemsa.gov.ng/directory/). For equipment standards, the Standards Organisation of Nigeria publishes information at [son.gov.ng](https://son.gov.ng/), but ask the installer which standards apply to the exact products quoted.

If goods do not match the agreed specification, the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2018 gives a right to return them for a full refund (section 122, as quoted by Templars in June 2025). That only helps if the specification is written down. Connecting a battery bank is not a DIY job, so leave the wiring to the qualified person who signs the contract.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much is a lithium battery in Nigeria?

Lagos sellers listed 5 kWh LiFePO4 batteries at N1,150,000 to N1,200,000 on 14 October 2026, and 10 kWh at N1,950,000 to N2,350,000. Prices move with the naira, so treat these as a snapshot. Ask for a current written quote that names the model.

### Can I put a lithium battery on my old inverter?

Sometimes, but it depends on the inverter model. Many older units are set up for lead-acid and may not match a lithium battery's voltage or management system. An installer should check this on site before you buy, and not guess from a photo.

### How long does a lithium battery last compared to tubular?

Felicity's manual states 6,000 cycles at 80% depth for its LPBF48100-A, against up to 1,500 for a Luminous tubular model, with a 5-year warranty on the Felicity. BusinessDay says lead-acid lasts three to five years. These are maker claims on their own test conditions, not promises for your house.

### Which should I buy if my budget is tight?

A tubular pair costs less upfront, about N520,000 against N1.15 to N1.2 million for 5 kWh of lithium, but you may buy it two to four times over ten years. If the battery is drained every night, lithium has a better case. If it is used lightly, tubular can be sensible, so get both options quoted.
